Air quality drift watch

rebuilt weekly

Models go stale. The world they were trained on moves on, and usually nobody notices until something downstream breaks. This one notices on its own, every week, with nobody watching it. The job: forecast how dirty a city's air will be (PM2.5, the soot fine enough to reach your bloodstream) a week ahead, from nothing but the weather forecast. When the model starts slipping it trains a replacement, then refuses to ship it unless it beats the one already in service. Six cities, each replayed week by week through a year of recorded data, from 2025 into 2026. One of them, Kraków, also runs for real every Monday.

One city at a time

Six cities, each with its own model and its own history of retraining. Every setting is identical across all of them, so where two cities behave differently it is their air that differs and not their tuning. Pick one, or see all six side by side.

Where the data comes from

One marker per city. Colour is how far its weather has moved from what its model was trained on, size is how long it was watched. Click a marker or a row to switch city.

What holds across all six

Nothing below depends on which city is selected. These are about the loop itself. The all-cities view puts the six on shared axes.

Why six models and not one?

The cheaper arrangement would be one model for all six cities, trained once and never retrained. It is scored here beside each city's own model.

A seven-day exam certifies a model for a month

Every promotion was decided by one exam: a week of air neither model had seen. This checks what each winner went on to deliver against the model it replaced.

Whether the detection works at all

In a real city nobody knows whether an alarm was right. So the same loop is run on a simulated city with two dials. One changes the weather. The other changes how weather turns into pollution. Each alarm should react to its own dial only.

Is this about the weather, or about a linear model?

Everything above uses one kind of model, a straight line through eight weather inputs. A straight line can follow a season only by being refitted, and that would look just like retraining paying off. So two cities are replayed with a model that can bend, tuned the same way: Delhi, where retraining pays most, and Los Angeles, the control.

How this works

The loop

One scheduled run, every week. Steps 3 and 4 only happen when step 2 says they should.

    Why two kinds of signal: the weather check needs no recorded outcomes, so it can speak first. But weather that looks different is not a model that is failing, so only a rise in the model's own error can order a retrain.

    Where it goes wrong: the error alarm compares the model with its own error at training time, and every promotion resets that bar. Models are promoted when the air is dirty, so the bar climbs, and past the seasonal peak the alarm cannot fire at all. Left alone that silenced Kraków for 30 weeks and Los Angeles for 35 of its 36. A second alarm now runs beside it, comparing the model with a plain daily profile of recent air, which no promotion can reset. With both, the longest silences are 22 weeks and 16.

    Five fixes were built, and none makes the forecasts better. Each was replayed on all six cities, on the loop as it first shipped and again on this one, with the expected results written down first. The second alarm stayed, for the silences it shortens: on nine cities this page does not show, it shortened them wherever it fired and did not change accuracy measurably. A longer exam, a schedule of re-examinations and an undo step change nothing measurable.

    One result did not survive the second run. A harder exam made two cities worse on the first loop and one on this one, which is within what chance produces across this many comparisons. What holds on both: winners deliver less than their exam promised. A year of weekly checks in one city holds only a handful of independent observations, so a fix worth a few percent would not show in either run.

    The same loop, running on a schedule

    The model

    It answers one question: given the weather forecast for a week from now, how dirty will the air be? It sees the forecast as it was issued, not the weather that turned out, and it is never told how dirty the air has been lately. It is simple on purpose: a simple model fails visibly when the world shifts, and the subject here is the loop around the model.
